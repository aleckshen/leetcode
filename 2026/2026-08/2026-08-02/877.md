We label the indicies of each element in the array from [1, 2, 3..., n]. If we take the sum of stones with odd indicies and the sum of stones with even indicies we notice that one will always be greater than the other since the sum of all stones is odd. We can then notice that the starting player will always be able to force either the sum of stones with odd inidcies or the sum of stones with even indicies. Say the larger sum exists with the sum of stones with odd indicies, then Alice will pick from the first stone in `piles` with odd index `1`. The next choice for the Bob will be either the stone with index `2` or the stones with index `n` which are both even. If this continues Alice will always be able to pick a stone with odd index and win, this will also be true for Alice if she decides to start from an even indexed stone (index `n`) and force Bob to pick odd indexed stones only.
